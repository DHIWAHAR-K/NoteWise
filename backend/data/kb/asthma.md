# Asthma

## Definition
Chronic inflammatory airway disease characterized by variable airflow obstruction, airway hyperresponsiveness, and symptoms of wheezing, dyspnea, chest tightness, and cough.

## GINA Severity Classification
| Step | Frequency of symptoms | Preferred controller |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (Mild intermittent) | < 2 days/week | PRN low-dose ICS-formoterol |
| 2 (Mild persistent) | > 2 days/week | Low-dose ICS daily |
| 3 (Moderate persistent) | Daily | Low-dose ICS + LABA |
| 4 (Severe persistent) | Continuous | Medium/high-dose ICS + LABA |
| 5 | Uncontrolled on Step 4 | Add tiotropium, biologics (dupilumab, omalizumab) |

## Rescue therapy
- **SABA** (albuterol/salbutamol MDI) 2 puffs PRN — not for daily use
- **ICS-formoterol** as both maintenance and reliever (MART)

## Acute exacerbation management
1. Supplemental O2 to maintain SpO2 ≥ 94%
2. SABA 4–8 puffs via MDI + spacer q20min × 3, then q1–4h PRN
3. Systemic corticosteroids: prednisone 40–60 mg/day × 5–7 days
4. Consider ipratropium bromide in moderate-severe exacerbations
5. Magnesium sulfate 2 g IV if severe, unresponsive

## Monitoring
- Spirometry (FEV1, FEV1/FVC) at diagnosis and every 1–2 years
- Peak flow monitoring during exacerbations
- Asthma Control Test (ACT) score at each visit
